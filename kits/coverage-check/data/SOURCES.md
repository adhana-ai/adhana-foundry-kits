# Where this corpus came from

**It was written here.** `tools/build_corpus.py` generates every warranty claim record from a
fixed seed (`SEED = 20260822`), so the corpus is byte-identical on every rebuild and there is no
third-party data in this kit at all.

| | |
|---|---|
| source | generated by `tools/build_corpus.py`, seed `20260822` |
| dataset version | `coverage-check-2026-08-22-55claims` |
| licence | **MIT** — this repository's own licence |
| claims | 55, in `data/corpus/*.txt`, 43,783 bytes |
| gold | 55 rows in `data/gold.jsonl` |
| claims / vehicles / dealers / platforms | invented identifiers, invented dealership names and invented platform labels, with no link to any real manufacturer, dealer, vehicle or customer |

## No real manufacturer, model, VIN, dealer or warranty booklet appears anywhere

**This is important enough to say before anything else.** Every coverage plan, month limit, mileage
limit, component list, labor operation code, dealership name and platform label in this kit was
invented for this corpus. No manufacturer's published warranty terms, service contract, component
schedule or labor operation catalogue was consulted, and none is reproduced. The vehicles are
"Platform C1 compact sedan" and five siblings; the identifiers are `VEH-` codes with no checksum
and no meaning; the dealers are names like "Ironwood Auto Group".

"Coverage plan", "in-service date", "powertrain", "labor operation", "wear item" and "claim status"
are ordinary warranty-administration vocabulary. The *terms* attached to them here are this kit's
own construction, chosen to be a plausible SHAPE — a bumper-to-bumper term, a longer powertrain
term, a longer-still emissions term, a purchased extended contract — and nobody's actual programme.

## Why an invented set of claims, when real ones exist

**Because a real dealer warranty claim is not something this repo can publish either.** It names a
real customer's vehicle, a real dealership, a technician's own words about a specific repair, and a
payment. There is no public corpus of (warranty claim, correct coverage decision) pairs, for the
same reason there is no public corpus of bank statements or proof-of-delivery records.

## The mechanic: one call per claim, fourteen fields

Each record states a claim id, the servicing dealer, the vehicle, the coverage plan, the in-service
date, the repair date, the odometer reading, the failed component, the claimed labor operation, the
coded cause, whether the claim has already been paid, and the technician's own free-text repair
narrative. The model returns fourteen fields — everything except the servicing dealer, which no
field maps to and which is therefore never sent — including the three it has to work out:
`months_in_service` (a calculation), `narrative_finding` (a reading) and `covered` (the rule). See
`src/prompt.py`.

## The coverage terms, invented

```
basic       36 months /  36,000 miles   every component below except wear items
powertrain  60 months /  60,000 miles   transmission_assembly, engine_short_block, drive_axle
emissions   96 months /  80,000 miles   catalytic_converter, oxygen_sensor, evap_canister
extended    84 months / 100,000 miles   every component below except wear items

wear items  brake_pads, wiper_blades, clutch_disc  -- on NO plan's component list
```

## The rule, and the six ways of getting it wrong

```
covered(plan, months, miles, component, labor_op, narrative_finding):
    1. narrative_finding is an exclusion            -> no    (outranks every coverage term)
    2. labor_op is not this component's operation   -> no    (not payable as coded)
    3. component is a wear item                     -> yes iff plan is basic/extended
                                                             AND months <= 12 AND miles <= 12000
    4. component not on this plan's component list  -> no
    5. months > plan's months OR miles > plan's miles -> no  (THE PLAN'S OWN limit, inclusive)
    6. otherwise                                    -> yes
```

Five things about that rule are stated to the model in full, because each is a reading a model —
or a person — falls into on its own:

| | |
|---|---|
| **an exclusion the technician describes outranks everything** | collision damage, an unauthorized modification or a missed maintenance interval denies the claim however new the vehicle is |
| **the coded `Cause Code` is not the evidence** | it is what the dealer coded; the narrative is what the technician found, and on **12 of these 55 claims** the two disagree |
| **the limit is the plan's own limit** | **10 covered claims are past 36 months AND 36,000 miles** — a reader who reaches for "the 3/36" denies every one of them |
| **a wear item is not automatically "no"** | 4 wear claims are covered as premature failures inside 12 months and 12,000 miles; 4 more are the same components just past the window |
| **`months_in_service` is a calculation** | complete months, minus one when the repair day-of-month is earlier than the in-service day-of-month. **6 claims sit exactly on a limit** (covered) and **7 sit exactly one month or one mile past it** (denied) |

The rule lives in one function — `tools/build_corpus.py::coverage_verdict()` and
`src/extract.py::coverage_verdict()` are the same six branches, used to write gold and to re-derive
it from the model's own reply — and is stated to the model by `src/prompt.py`, so the kit cannot
drift about what "covered" means. `evals/check_labels.py` asserts the two copies agree before
anything may spend.

## The two planted confusions, both in the technician narrative

The narrative is by design the loudest and longest thing on the record. It carries three sentences:
what the technician found, what they did, and **their own guess about whether the claim will pay**.

**1. The closing opinion.** On exactly **22 of 55 claims (40%)** that guess contradicts the rule — a
covered claim signed off "Vehicle is well outside the 3/36 so I expect this one gets denied", or a
denied one signed off "Should be covered under the plan terms, no question." Anything that reads
the opinion instead of applying the terms — including `evals/baseline.py`, deliberately — fails
those claims by construction.

**2. The coded cause against the narrated finding.** On **12 claims** the structured `Cause Code`
disagrees with what the narrative describes, in both directions:

- **6 exclusions coded `defect`** — a drive axle "cracked open with fresh impact marks … a strike
  from underneath, not a failure", filed as a defect;
- **6 plain internal failures coded as damage, a modification or missed maintenance** — so a reader
  who learns "the coded field is always wrong" is no better off than one who trusts it.

Gold's `covered` is **never** derived from either the opinion or the coded cause. It is the
six-branch rule, run over the same values the record itself states.

## The composition is exact, then shuffled — not drawn per record

Each class is a fixed **count**, shuffled by the seeded RNG, rather than a per-record coin. A
sibling kit's first generator asked for 40% ambiguity and delivered 51%; a count 1.7 standard
deviations off its own design is sampling noise being published as a corpus property. The exclusion
*kinds* are dealt the same way, after a first draft came out 5 / 2 / 1 and would have published
"collision damage coded as a defect" as the headline confusion on the strength of a single record.

The mix on this seed:

| | |
|---|---|
| covered | 27 |
| not covered | 28 |
| — decided by an exclusion in the narrative | 8 (3 collision damage, 3 unauthorized modification, 2 missed maintenance) |
| — decided by a labor operation that is not the component's | 3 |
| — decided by the wear-item window | 8 (4 covered as premature failures, 4 denied) |
| — decided by the plan's component list | 6 |
| — decided by the plan's month or mileage limit | 7 |
| — inside every term | 23 |
| covered claims past 36 months **and** 36,000 miles (the 3/36 trap) | 10 |
| exactly on a month or mileage limit (inclusive → covered) | 6 |
| technician opinion contradicting the verdict | 22 (40%) |
| coded cause disagreeing with the narrated finding | 12 |
| not covered **and** already paid — the recovery flag | 16 |

`evals/check_labels.py` re-runs the rule and the date arithmetic over every gold row before a run
is allowed to spend, and fails if any label disagrees with its own values. It separately asserts
that every branch is exercised by at least two rows, that the wear exception cuts both ways, that
the coded-cause confusion runs in both directions, and that every opinion template classifies to
the register it was authored in against the free floor's own keyword list — the exact lesson a
sibling kit paid for live when a keyword fired on a negation.

## The guardrail is a business condition, not a check on the model

`src/extract.py::compute()` routes a claim for a recovery review when it is not covered **and** the
claim has already been paid to the dealer. An uncovered claim still in `submitted` can simply be
denied before any money moves; the same claim already paid means the money is out the door.

⚠︎ **This is the kit's own simplification, not any manufacturer's warranty-recovery policy.** No
published warranty programme, dealer agreement, audit procedure or chargeback rule was consulted,
and none is reproduced. A real warranty desk weighs the dollar value of the claim, the dealer's own
claim history and any audit or appeal window; this is two booleans, chosen because it is the
smallest rule that is genuinely useful and readable off one reply.

## What it cannot tell you

⚠︎ **This kit adjudicates and routes only — it never denies a claim, opens a chargeback, debits a
dealer or contacts anybody.** `covered` is an extracted field and `needs_review` is a routing
signal; nothing here disposes of anything.

⚠︎ **An invented corpus contains the confusions we thought to plant and no others.** A real warranty
queue carries confusions this one does not attempt — a goodwill or policy-adjustment authorisation
outside the written terms, a recall or service campaign that supersedes coverage entirely, a
component replaced under a previous claim and failing again, a transferred or lapsed extended
contract, a vehicle whose in-service date was corrected after the fact, sublet and parts-only
lines, or a narrative written in a technician's shorthand rather than in sentences. A model that
clears this corpus has cleared what this corpus tests, not every way a coverage decision can go
wrong.
