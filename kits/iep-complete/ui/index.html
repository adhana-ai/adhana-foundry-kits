Check a plan for the components the rules require — and for the ones that are there and cannot be measured

One education plan, one model call, a state for every component in the shipped rulebook — plus a worklist decision taken afterwards, in pure code, from two of the extracted values. The second state is the point. A goal with no baseline, no criterion or no measurement method passes every checkbox review and helps nobody, and it is much harder to see than a missing heading. The previous reviewer’s own checklist and the case manager’s note are both on the plan, and neither decides anything.

This produces a reviewer’s worklist. It never approves, signs, files or amends a plan. Nothing here is a decision about a pupil; the team that writes the plan decides what goes in it. The component rulebook shipped with this kit is invented and is not an authority — it was written for this kit and reproduces no statute, regulation, agency guidance or plan template, and its transition age is a number this kit chose. Replace it with your own before you review anything real by it. Every plan in this corpus is generated. No real pupil, school or district appears anywhere: there are no names, no dates and no diagnosis, and each file says so on its own first line.

The worklist flag is a business condition, not a check on the model: on_worklist fires when plan_outcome is anything other than complete and plan_status is in_effect. A draft can simply be finished before it is signed; the same finding on a plan already in effect means a pupil is being taught against a goal nobody can measure, and that is the row to open first. undetermined counts here too — treating an unknown as a pass is the one thing a completeness check must never do. This is the kit’s own simplification, not anybody’s review policy — no district procedure, agency guidance or regulation was consulted, and none is reproduced.
