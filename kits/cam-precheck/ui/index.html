Check one CAM reconciliation line against the tenant’s own lease terms

One operating-expense line, one model call, a field table — plus a routing decision taken afterwards, in pure code, from three of the extracted values. What the lease permits is four stages of arithmetic in a fixed order: what of the pool is billable at all, then the gross-up for a partly-vacant building, then the tenant’s weighted pro-rata share, then the cap. Two things on the page argue with that arithmetic and neither is evidence — the property accountant’s own note, and what the expense happens to be called.

The flag is a business condition, not a check on the model: needs_review fires when the line is billed wrong, the error runs against the tenant, and statement_status is issued. A wrong line still in draft can simply be corrected before it is released — that is what a pre-check is for; the same error already issued has reached the tenant, and an overcharge is what a tenant’s auditor claws back. It reads a computed field, so it inherits every arithmetic error above — the last row re-runs the four stages on whatever the reply itself returned, which catches a reply that contradicts its own numbers and is blind to one that is wrong and consistent. This is the kit’s own simplification, not a real property-accounting policy — no lease’s audit-rights clause or landlord’s release procedure was consulted, and none is reproduced.
