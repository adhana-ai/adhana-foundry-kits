One alert review sheet, one model call, a field table — plus an adjudication taken afterwards, in pure code, from eight of the extracted values. The verdict is an identifier comparison with a stopping order: is a strong identifier of the same type on both records and equal, is one there and different, then does a comparable moderate identifier conflict, then do enough of them agree — and otherwise the file does not decide. The names, the nationalities, the screening engine’s own match score and the analyst’s note are all on the sheet, and none of them decides anything.

This kit clears nothing, blocks nothing and files nothing. No alert is closed, no party is designated or de-designated, no account is frozen or released, no payment is stopped or let through, and no report of any kind is made to anybody. It proposes an adjudication with the deciding identifier attached, names what it could not determine, and a human makes the call.

The rulebook shipped with this kit is illustrative and is not an authority — it was written for this kit and reproduces no sanctions programme, designation list, supervisor’s guidance or vendor tuning. Every name, list, programme, country, place and identifier in this corpus is invented, and no real list, authority, programme, jurisdiction or person is named anywhere in it. Replace the rulebook with your own before you decide anything real by it.

The escalation flag is a business condition, not a check on the model: needs_escalation fires when verdict is anything other than not_a_match and account_status is live. A pending onboarding can wait for a person to work the alert; the same verdict on a live account means money is moving for a party nobody has adjudicated. insufficient_information counts here too — an alert nobody can decide, on an account that is already running, is precisely the one to put in front of a human, and treating “cannot tell” as a clearance is the worst thing anything in this kit could do. It orders a worklist and nothing else — it does not freeze the account, stop a payment, close or escalate a case, or notify anybody. And it is this kit’s own simplification, not anybody’s real alert-handling policy — no supervisor’s guidance, institution’s procedure or regulatory rule was consulted, and none is reproduced.
