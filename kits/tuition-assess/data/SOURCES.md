# Where this corpus came from

**It was written here.** `tools/build_corpus.py` generates every student-account tuition assessment
from a fixed seed (`SEED = 20260822`), so the corpus is byte-identical on every rebuild and there
is no third-party data in this kit at all.

| | |
|---|---|
| source | generated by `tools/build_corpus.py`, seed `20260822` |
| dataset version | `tuition-assess-2026-08-22-55records` |
| licence | **MIT** — this repository's own licence |
| records | 55, in `data/corpus/*.txt`, 28,566 bytes |
| gold | 55 rows in `data/gold.jsonl` |
| accounts / campuses / terms / waivers | invented identifiers, invented campus names, an invented rate table and invented bursar notes, with no link to any real institution, schedule or student |

## ⚠︎ No real student data, and none of it is derived from any

Tuition assessment is an **education-records** domain. A real record names a real student, their
enrolment, their aid, and often their residency history — none of which belongs in a public
repository under any licence. **Nothing in this corpus is drawn from, sampled from, anonymised out
of or paraphrased from a real student account.** Every account id, campus name, term code, credit
load, waiver, dollar figure and bursar note was written by the generator from a seed.

**No real institution, real published tuition schedule, real fee bill, real waiver programme or
real residency regulation is named or reproduced anywhere in this kit.** "Residency tier",
"enrolled credits", "course level", "differential fee", "mandatory fee" and "waiver" are ordinary
student-billing vocabulary; the numbers attached to them here are this kit's own invention.

## Why an invented set of assessments, when real ones exist

Because a real student-account tuition assessment is not something this repository can publish
either, and there is no public corpus of (assessment record, correctly-assessed-total) pairs — for
the same reason there is no public corpus of bank statements or proof-of-delivery records. The
alternative to writing one is not using a real one; it is not shipping the kit.

## The mechanic: one call per record, twelve fields

Each record states a student account, its campus, the term, the residency tier of record, the
enrolled credit load, the course level, any waiver on the account, the total the bursar system
actually assessed, whether the bill has posted, any residency action on file, and the bursar's own
free-text note. The model extracts twelve fields — everything except the campus, which no field
maps to and which is therefore never sent — including the two it has to decide: whether the
assessed total is what the rate table entitles the account to, and, when it is not, **which single
step of the table it departs from**. See `src/prompt.py`.

## The rate table, and the four steps a model reading it casually gets wrong

```
correct_total(residency_tier, enrolled_credits, course_level, waiver_type):

  a. TUITION        credits >= 12  ->  flat term rate   In-State 4600   Out-of-State 13200
                    credits <  12  ->  credits x        In-State  410   Out-of-State  1180
  b. DIFFERENTIAL   credits x  Lower Division 0   Upper Division 38   Graduate 65
  c. MANDATORY FEE  full-time 612                part-time 306
  d. WAIVER, LAST   None                          waives nothing
                    Employee Tuition Remission    100 pct of (a) only
                    Staff Dependent Waiver         50 pct of (a) only
                    Regents Fee Waiver            100 pct of (a) AND 100 pct of (c)

  total = (a) + (b) + (c) - (d)          NO waiver ever reduces (b).
```

Four things about that table are stated to the model in full, because each is a reading a model
falls into on its own:

| | |
|---|---|
| **the full-time threshold is inclusive, and the flat band is cheaper** | exactly 12 credits is full-time, so tuition is the flat rate — **not** 12 times the per-credit rate. The two differ by $320 in-state and $960 out-of-state, so reading the band wrong is worth money rather than being a rounding difference. **13 of these 55 records sit exactly on the threshold** and 7 sit one credit under it |
| **a mid-term reclassification does not reprice this term** | **20 records carry a residency reclassification** effective after the term's census date. It applies from the *following* term. **6 of them were assessed at the new tier anyway** — that is the fault — and **14 carry the same line correctly ignored**, so "a reclassification is on file" is a decoy rather than a giveaway |
| **the differential fee is zero for Lower Division and per credit for the other two** | and it is charged on top of the flat full-time band as well as on top of a part-time load. Being full-time does not fold it in |
| **a waiver that does not cover a charge is not an error** | three of the four waivers cover base tuition only. **None of the four ever touches the differential fee.** A model that "corrects" an account because the mandatory fee survived a tuition waiver is wrong about the account, not about the fee |

The table lives in one function — `tools/build_corpus.py::assess()` and `src/extract.py::assess()`
are the same arithmetic, used to write gold and to re-derive it from the model's own reply — and is
stated to the model by `src/prompt.py` and `data/fields.json`, so the corpus, the prompt and the
guardrail cannot drift apart about what a correct assessment is.

## Every variance has exactly one explanation, and that is asserted per record

`variance_reason` is only an honest field if a mis-assessed total has one true cause. So each
mis-assessed record is built by re-running the same `assess()` with **exactly one** decision
substituted, and then `_verify()` checks that **no other** single substitution reproduces the same
number. A record whose total two different single mistakes could both explain is re-drawn under the
seed rather than shipped with an arbitrary label; **one re-draw** was needed on this seed, and the
build prints the count.

`evals/check_labels.py` re-runs that check independently before a run is allowed to spend, along
with the arithmetic behind every gold label.

## The second planted decoy: correctness is arithmetic, and the note disagrees with it

`N_AMBIGUOUS = 22` puts a bursar note from the wrong register on exactly 40 pct of records:

- a genuinely mis-assessed account carrying a breezy note — *"Charges look normal for this
  enrollment pattern, all in order."* — while the arithmetic says otherwise;
- a correctly assessed account carrying a note that reads like a flagged problem — *"Escalated last
  term for a possible mis-assessment -- pending bursar review."*

Gold's `assessment_correct` is **never** derived from the note. It is the rate table, run over the
same values the record itself states. `evals/check_labels.py` separately asserts that every note
template classifies to the register it was written as, against the free floor's own keyword list,
before anything may spend — a sibling kit earlier in this series shipped a keyword that fired on a
negation inside a breezy note and mis-registered four records for days.

## The composition is exact, then shuffled — not drawn per record

Several sibling kits in this series draw each record's class from an independent coin, which is
fine at 500 records and is not fine at 55. So each class here is a fixed **count**, shuffled by the
seeded RNG, and the credit loads at and beside the threshold are **designed** rather than drawn: no
record outside the two boundary groups is allowed 11 or 12 credits, so the two numbers on the page
are the two numbers the design asked for.

The mix on this seed:

| | |
|---|---|
| correctly assessed | 28 |
| mis-assessed | 27 |
| — credit band (the flat/per-credit choice) | 8 |
| — residency tier (the reclassification applied a term early) | 6 |
| — differential fee (wrong course level's rate) | 6 |
| — waiver coverage (reached a charge it does not cover, or missed one it does) | 4 |
| — mandatory fee (per-term fee at the wrong band) | 3 |
| at exactly 12 credits — the full-time threshold | 13 |
| at exactly 11 credits — one under it | 7 |
| carrying a mid-term residency reclassification | 20 (6 wrongly applied, 14 correctly ignored) |
| bursar note from the contradicting register | 22 (40 pct) |
| mis-assessed **and** already posted — the review flag | 14 |
| course level: Lower Division / Upper Division / Graduate | 18 / 16 / 21 |
| waiver: None / Employee / Staff Dependent / Regents | 15 / 12 / 14 / 14 |

**The variances are not all large.** Measured off gold: the smallest is **$110**, the median
**$320** and the largest **$8,600**. A kit whose planted errors are all four-figure is measuring
whether a model can spot a big number, not whether it can run a rate table.

## The guardrail is a business condition, not a check on the model

`src/extract.py::compute()` routes an account for a bursar correction when the assessed total is
wrong **and** the bill has already posted. A mis-assessed account still in draft can simply be
corrected before it posts; the same variance on a posted bill means a corrected bill or a refund
against a balance the student may already have paid.

⚠︎ **This is the kit's own simplification, not any institution's billing-adjustment policy.** No
published schedule, regulation or bursar procedure was consulted, and none is reproduced. A real
office weighs the dollar size of the variance, whether aid has disbursed against it, and any
refund-deadline rule; this is two booleans, chosen because it is the smallest rule that is
genuinely useful and readable off one reply.

## What it cannot tell you

⚠︎ **This kit checks and routes only — it never re-bills an account, reverses a charge, adjusts aid
or contacts a student.** `assessment_correct` and `variance_reason` are extracted fields and
`needs_review` is a routing signal; nothing here disposes of anything.

⚠︎ **An invented corpus contains the ambiguity we thought to plant and no others.** A real bursar
queue carries confusions this one does not attempt — a student who drops below the threshold after
the census date, a programme on its own board-approved rate outside the standard bands, two waivers
stacked on one account with an ordering rule between them, a course-by-course differential rather
than one level for the whole load, or a term-to-term proration on a partial withdrawal. A model
that clears this corpus has cleared what this corpus tests, not every way a tuition assessment can
go wrong.

⚠︎ **Every record here has exactly one thing wrong with it, at most.** That is what makes
`variance_reason` a scoreable field, and it is also the least realistic thing about the corpus: a
real mis-assessment often has two causes at once, and nothing here measures what the model does
then.
