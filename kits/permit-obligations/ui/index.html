Which permit obligations need action, by when, and which cannot be determined

One site’s obligation register, one model call, a row per condition — plus a status decided afterwards, in pure code, from the values that came back. The rule reads five things in order: is the register readable, does the condition still bind, has the trigger fired, what is the due date, and where does that date sit against the register date and the type’s own action window. The site’s own register flag is on every row, is copied out, and decides nothing.

This kit watches nothing. It reads one register that somebody else assembled, as at the date printed on it, and proposes a worklist. It does not poll, subscribe, schedule, alert, escalate, file, submit, renew or clear, and nothing in it runs unattended. It proposes a status with its reasoning and names what it could not determine; it never authorises, files or clears anything. A qualified person reads the permit. The rulebook shipped with this kit is illustrative and is not an authority — it was written for this kit and reproduces no permit, licence condition, regulator’s guidance or statutory schedule. Replace it with your own before you put a single obligation on a worklist by it.

The escalation flag is a business condition, not a check on the model: it fires when at least one obligation the rulebook makes overdue carries a register flag of on track or closed. An overdue row already flagged attention is a site that knows about it. An overdue row the site’s own tracker calls fine is the other thing entirely — nobody is looking, and chasing the site’s own list will never surface it. This is the kit’s own simplification, not any real escalation policy — no permit, regulator’s guidance or operator’s procedure was consulted, and none is reproduced.
