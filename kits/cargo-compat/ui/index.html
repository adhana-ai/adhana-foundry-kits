Check a tank’s prior cargo against the product about to go in

One pre-load check sheet, one model call, a field table — plus a hold decision taken afterwards, in pure code, from two of the extracted values. The verdict is a compatibility-matrix lookup with a stopping order: is the prior cargo even recorded, is the pair reactive, is the predecessor banned for this grade over its own look-back depth, and only then did the certificate meet the minimum wash. The tank log’s own “wash performed” line and the inspector’s note are both on the sheet, and neither decides anything.

This proposes a verdict. It never authorises a load. A qualified person authorises the load, against the incoming product’s safety data sheet and the tank’s real cleaning record. The compatibility matrix shipped with this kit is illustrative and is not an authority — it was written for this kit and reproduces no commercial, industry-body or proprietary chart. Replace it with your own before you decide anything real by it.

The hold flag is a business condition, not a check on the model: needs_hold fires when verdict is anything other than accept and load_status is loaded. A pending load can simply be cleaned or refused before it goes in; the same verdict on a tank already loaded means product is sitting on a residue it should not be sitting on. undetermined counts here too — treating an unknown as a pass is the one thing a pre-load check must never do. This is the kit’s own simplification, not a terminal’s quarantine policy — no operator’s procedure, industry code or regulatory rule was consulted, and none is reproduced.
