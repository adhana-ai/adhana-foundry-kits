Decide whether a record series past its retention is still frozen by a hold

One records-disposition review, one model call, a field table — plus a routing decision taken afterwards, in pure code, from two of the extracted values. Eligibility is a three-condition derivation with a priority order: a live hold first, then a longer-retention overlapping series, then the series’ own retention. The hard part is the first one, because a hold’s scope is prose — “all correspondence relating to the Riverside project, 2019 onward” — and it has to be judged against this record’s own category, project and closed date. The records officer’s note is their remark on the series, and on this corpus it often reads the opposite way from what the registry says.

This proposes; a records officer releases. There is no destroy control here and no write path of any kind. “Eligible” means may be put in front of a person for approval, never destroy it — and the flag at the bottom asks for a series to be pulled back out of a destruction queue.

The flag is a business condition, not a check on the model: needs_review fires when disposition_eligible is no and queue_status is queued. A frozen series nobody has queued can be left where it is until the next cycle; the same series already in the queue is one approval away from being destroyed under a live hold, so somebody has to pull it out today. Note the direction — this flag never proposes a destruction. It is the kit’s own simplification, not a real records programme’s escalation policy — no published general records schedule, disposition authority or hold procedure was consulted, and none is reproduced.
