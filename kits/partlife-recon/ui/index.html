Reconcile a life-limited part’s hours and cycles against its record trail

One component record pack, one model call, a field table — plus an escalation decision taken afterwards, in pure code, from three of the extracted values. The accumulated life is not printed anywhere on the pack: it is the sum of every installation period, with hours and cycles added up separately because each period ran at a different ratio, with an overhaul resetting time since overhaul and never time since new, and with any period whose records are missing contributing nothing at all. The figures on the component’s own tag are a claim to be reconciled against that, not a shortcut past it.

This reconciles records. It does not release anything to service. Nothing on this page is an airworthiness determination, a life-remaining certificate or a release. It reconstructs what the trail substantiates, names every discrepancy and every gap, and escalates.

The flag is a business condition, not a check on the model: escalate fires when the pack carries a discrepancy — the trail does not put the component inside both limits, or the component’s own tag disagrees with the trail — and disposition_requested is return to service. The same discrepancy on a component headed for shelf storage still has to be resolved; it does not have to be resolved before an aircraft moves. This is the kit’s own simplification, not any operator’s or authority’s release procedure — no airworthiness regulation or maintenance-organisation exposition was consulted, and none is reproduced. A no here means this rule found nothing to raise. It does not mean the component may fly.
