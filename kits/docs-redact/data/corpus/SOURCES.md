# Corpus sources — docs-redact

**18 documents, all original work, authored specifically for this kit.** MIT (same as the rest of
the repo). No third-party content, no scraped source, nothing to attribute.

No real person's data appears anywhere in this corpus. Every name, Social Security Number, date of
birth, email address, phone number, mailing address and card number is fabricated for this purpose
— generated by `tools/build_corpus.py` from a table of invented entities, never sourced from a real
individual, a real institution's actual customer, or any dataset.

## Why self-authored rather than fetched

The other kits in this repo build their corpora from real public records — ClinicalTrials.gov study
records, Federal Register filings — because the property under test is *reading comprehension of a
real document*. This kit is the opposite case: the property under test is whether a detector finds
real people's sensitive data, so testing it against real people's sensitive data is the one thing
this kit must never do. A corpus that is invented end to end is not a compromise here — it is the
only correct choice.

## What was authored, and why 18 different shapes

Eighteen document "kinds" — HR letters, customer-support and cancellation emails, a medical intake
form, insurance and claims correspondence, an invoice, a bank statement notice, a lease notice, a
gym and a school enrollment form, a warranty registration, a car rental agreement, a mortgage
application summary, a jury duty summons, a background-check report, a utility bill — so the
detector is tested against a variety of house styles rather than one template with the names
swapped. No two documents share a structure.

## Category coverage and format variety

Each document carries 3–7 fabricated spans across the seven categories this kit detects (`NAME`,
`SSN`, `EMAIL`, `PHONE`, `ADDRESS`, `DOB`, `CARD`). Every category appears in multiple documents, in
different formats, so a detector that only recognises one spelling of a category is measurably worse
than one that generalises:

| category | documents | format variety seen |
|---|---|---|
| NAME | all 18 | — |
| SSN | 7 | dashed (`219-08-4471`), run together (`219084471`) |
| EMAIL | 14 | corporate and personal domains |
| PHONE | 16 | parenthesised (`(415) 555-0138`), dashed, dotted, country-prefixed (`+1 860-555-0121`) |
| ADDRESS | 15 | full street/city/state/ZIP, one line |
| DOB | 11 | `MM/DD/YYYY`, `YYYY-MM-DD`, `12 Nov 1975`, `March 22, 1990` |
| CARD | 6 | space-grouped, dash-grouped, and Amex-style 4-6-5 grouping |

## Where the labels come from

`data/labelled.jsonl` is **not** hand-typed against the finished documents. `tools/build_corpus.py`
holds one dict of named entities per document and renders the document text from it; the labelled
spans are read back out of the exact same dict, so a document's text and its labels cannot drift
apart the way two independently authored files can. Every span's `text` is verified to be an exact
substring of its document — see the kit's build verification, which fails loudly rather than
shipping a label that does not literally appear in the text it claims to label.

## Reproducing this corpus

```bash
python -m tools.build_corpus     # deterministic — same 18 documents and labels every time
```
